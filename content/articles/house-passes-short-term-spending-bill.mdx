The House passed a 72-hour stopgap spending bill Tuesday evening, sending the measure to the Senate as lawmakers raced to avoid a midnight government shutdown that would have shuttered federal agencies and disrupted paychecks for millions of Americans.

## A last-minute scramble on the floor

The vote was 312 to 102, with dozens of Republicans joining nearly every Democrat to support the short-term patch. Speaker of the House Mike Johnson (R-La.) brought the bill to the floor under suspension of the rules, a maneuver that usually requires two-thirds support and signals that GOP leaders could not pass it with their own majority.

<blockquote>"We are not out of the woods yet, but the American people can breathe a little easier tonight," Johnson told reporters after the vote.</blockquote>

The bill funds the government through Friday at current levels and includes no new policy riders — a concession to Democrats who had demanded a "clean" extension.

## What happens next

Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) said he would bring the bill to a vote Wednesday morning and predicted it would pass with bipartisan support:

- The Senate will vote on the CR Wednesday at 10 a.m.
- President Biden has said he will sign it immediately.
- Negotiators will continue talks on a longer-term omnibus.

### Key sticking points

Negotiators are still at odds over border-security funding, disaster relief for hurricane-ravaged states, and a controversial provision that would restrict access to certain abortion medications in federal clinics.

## What a shutdown would have meant

A midnight shutdown would have furloughed hundreds of thousands of federal employees, closed national parks, and paused safety inspections at airports. The Office of Management and Budget had already instructed agencies to prepare contingency plans.
