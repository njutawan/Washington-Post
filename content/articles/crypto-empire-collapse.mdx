For much of the last year, customers of the crypto exchange FTX Global thought their funds were safe. Regulators now allege that more than $8 billion in customer deposits was secretly diverted to an affiliated trading firm, using a software "backdoor" executives spent years deliberately hiding from auditors.

## How the scheme allegedly worked

Prosecutors say a small group of engineers built a hidden feature into the exchange's accounting software that allowed the trading firm, Alameda Research, to withdraw funds from customer accounts without recording the liabilities on FTX's books.

- The backdoor was hidden in a module named "online_prices," according to the indictment.
- Auditors were given doctored screenshots of the dashboard showing no negative balances.
- Funds were converted to a custom token that the firm could print at will.

> "This isn't just a case of mismanagement," one federal investigator told The Post. "This was a years-long fraud dressed up as a tech company."

## What happens to customers now

Roughly 1.2 million creditors are owed money, according to bankruptcy filings. A court-appointed trustee has recovered about $3.4 billion in assets so far, largely from crypto wallets controlled by the founder.

Customers who lost less than $10,000 are expected to be made nearly whole under a proposed settlement, but larger depositors could recoup less than 30 cents on the dollar.

## What regulators missed

Critics say the Commodity Futures Trading Commission was warned about accounting irregularities more than a year before the collapse, but failed to act because of jurisdictional fights with the Securities and Exchange Commission.
